# Reducing Supplier Dependency in a Small Satellite-Connectivity Business

**Status:** Prototype sample — unpublished  
**Date:** 29 August 2026  
**Topic:** SATCOM · Technology Strategy · Digital Sovereignty

Small technology businesses can accumulate supplier dependency gradually. Collaboration tools, file storage, CRM, infrastructure and AI services may each be reasonable decisions in isolation, while the combined operating model creates increasing dependence on vendors, hosting jurisdictions and proprietary data flows.

For a satellite-connectivity SME, this matters beyond licence cost. The decision can affect data control, interoperability, resilience, internal ownership and the organisation’s ability to change suppliers later.

## Start with the operating model, not the software list

An open-source-first strategy should not begin with the assumption that open source is automatically cheaper or better. The more useful question is whether a different operating model would improve long-term control while remaining supportable by the organisation.

A practical assessment can compare options across:

- Functionality
- Hosting jurisdiction
- Implementation effort
- Support availability
- Interoperability
- Data portability
- Supplier dependency
- Internal ownership

This turns a software-selection exercise into a technology-strategy decision.

## Treat gross savings as a scenario, not an outcome

In one client-context proposal I developed, a three-year lifecycle scenario identified approximately **£5,000–£28,000 in potential gross savings**.

That range was not treated as realised value. Migration, implementation, support, training and operational-management costs could materially reduce or eliminate it.

The useful output was therefore not a headline saving figure. It was a decision framework showing the conditions under which a different operating model might become worthwhile.

## Supplier dependency is also a governance issue

A change in collaboration, CRM, infrastructure or AI services can alter:

- Who controls the data
- Which jurisdiction hosts it
- How easily it can be exported
- Who owns implementation knowledge
- How dependent operations become on a particular supplier
- What internal capability is required to manage the environment

For a small company, these factors can be more important than feature comparisons.

## Open source does not remove supplier risk

Open-source software can improve portability and transparency, but it can still be poorly documented, weakly supported or difficult to operate.

A maturity assessment therefore needs to consider more than whether source code is available. Relevant dimensions include longevity, product quality, maintenance activity, documentation, community and paid support, training, integration and professional-services availability.

## The strategic question

The useful question is not:

> “Can we replace proprietary products with open-source products?”

It is:

> “Which combination of software, hosting, support and internal ownership gives the organisation an acceptable balance of cost, resilience, control and operational capacity?”

That framing keeps digital sovereignty tied to business reality rather than ideology.
