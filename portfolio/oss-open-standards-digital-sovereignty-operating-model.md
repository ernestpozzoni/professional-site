# Open Source, Open Standards and Digital Sovereignty Operating Model Proposal

**2024 · Client-context initiative developed during the Link Communications Systems engagement**

[Back to Portfolio](README.md) · [Related CV entry](../README.md)

## Context or problem

During the Link Communications Systems engagement, I identified an opportunity to examine whether a small satellite-connectivity business could reduce supplier dependency, strengthen data control and improve long-term operational ownership through greater use of open-source software (OSS), open standards and UK- or EU-hosted services.

## Objective

Develop and assess an alternative operating model covering collaboration, file management, CRM, infrastructure and AI services while balancing:

- Cost
- Security
- Privacy
- Regulatory considerations
- Support requirements
- Organisational capacity

## My role

I designed the OSS-first operating-model proposal and the decision framework used to compare alternative arrangements.

The proposed environment combined:

- Managed Nextcloud
- UK- or EU-hosted infrastructure and services
- Open-source CRM
- Private or EU-hosted AI options

## Stakeholders or environment

The work was developed in the context of a **small, resource-constrained satellite-connectivity SME**.

The decision environment included management ownership, internal operational capacity and dependencies on external software, infrastructure, cloud, support and AI service suppliers.

## Approach and methods

### Option assessment

Compared service and supplier arrangements across:

- Functionality
- Hosting jurisdiction
- Implementation requirements
- Support availability
- Interoperability
- Data portability
- Supplier dependency
- Internal ownership

### Software assessment

Drew on the **Navica Open Source Maturity Model (N-OSMM)** and the broader OSS quality-assessment dimensions described by **Spinellis et al. (2009)**, considering:

- Functionality
- Longevity
- Product quality
- Development and maintenance activity
- Documentation
- Community and paid support
- Training
- Integration
- Professional-services availability

### Financial scenario

Built a three-year lifecycle scenario identifying approximately **£5,000–£28,000 in potential gross savings**.

The model explicitly distinguished these possible savings from:

- Implementation costs
- Migration costs
- Support costs
- Training costs
- Operational-management costs

### Governance assessment

Examined:

- Supplier governance
- Data control
- International data transfers
- Interoperability
- Implementation ownership
- Organisational capacity to operate or oversee the proposed environment

### Regulatory and security assessment

Connected the proposal with:

- UK GDPR
- International-transfer considerations
- PECR considerations
- NCSC Cloud Security Principles
- EU Data Act principles
- Potentially relevant NIS2 and UK cyber-resilience considerations for satellite-sector supply chains

## Actions and deliverables

Produced a structured transformation proposal and decision framework intended to enable management to compare:

- OSS options
- Managed-service options
- Conventional cloud options
- Supplier-dependent options

The comparison covered financial, operational, governance and regulatory dimensions.

## Evidence

The project produced inspectable evidence in the form of:

- A structured target operating-model proposal
- A multi-factor service and supplier option assessment
- An OSS maturity/readiness assessment approach
- A three-year financial scenario
- Governance and regulatory/security decision criteria

The quantified scenario identified approximately **£5,000–£28,000 in possible three-year gross savings**.

## Outcomes or lessons

The work created a quantified strategic option for strengthening digital sovereignty, resilience and operational control in a small satellite-connectivity business.

The main decision lesson was that nominal licence or service savings cannot be treated as realised value without also accounting for migration, implementation, support, training, governance and operational ownership.
